Spice 101

What am I eating?

New to Indian food? Here's a plain-language crash course. Ask us anything; that's the fun part.

MildestHottest
KormaAlmond, coconut and cardamom. Start here.
Butter MasalaTomato, cashew and cream. This is “butter chicken.”
Tikka MasalaCharred in the tandoor first, with more spice in the sauce.
Daal MakhaniBlack lentils cooked low overnight. Earthy.
HandiA creamy white curry with cashew and cardamom.
KadaiPeppers and onion. Thick, with a real kick.

Naan

Soft flatbread slapped onto the wall of a clay oven. Pillowy, a little charred and made for scooping.

Paratha

Dough folded over and over with butter, then pan-fried. Flaky, a bit like a savoury croissant.

Bhatura

Deep-fried bread that puffs up like a balloon. Tear it and scoop up the chana.

Kathi Roll

Paratha wrapped around grilled meat and onion. India's answer to the burrito.

Chaat

Street-snack style: crunchy and tangy, loaded with yogurt and tamarind. Messy on purpose.

Kulcha

Stuffed flatbread baked in the tandoor. The Amritsar version is crisp outside and soft inside.

Tandoor

A clay oven that runs over 400°C. Anything “tandoori” was charred in there on a skewer.

Tikka

Boneless pieces, marinated and grilled. Tikka is the cut, not a sauce.

Paneer

Fresh pressed cheese that holds its shape when cooked. Mild and squeaky, like halloumi.

Raita

Cool yogurt with cucumber. It puts out the fire better than water does.

Thaali

One tray of small bowls: curries, daal, rice, bread, raita and a sweet.

Lassi

A yogurt drink, sweet or salted. Mango is the crowd favourite.

How to order

One curry and one bread per person, one rice for the table, and something from the tandoor to share.

How to eat it

Tear the bread and scoop. Don't worry about being tidy. There's cutlery if you want it.

About heat

Every curry is made mild, medium or hot to order. Mild here really means mild.
